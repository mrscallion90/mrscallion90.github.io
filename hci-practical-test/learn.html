Learn

The basics, in plain English

Five ideas that cover most of what you need to know as you start out. Short enough to read on the way to class.

Five things worth knowing

Read one, or read all five. None of them take long.

Budgeting

A budget is a plan for your money, made before you spend it. It does not have to be strict. It has to be honest.

Start with what comes in, then list what must go out. What is left is yours to decide.

Saving

Saving works better as a habit than as a leftover. Decide the amount on payday, then move it before you spend.

A common guideline is to save 10% of your income. The habit matters more than the first amount.

Needs and wants

Needs keep your life running: rent, food, transport, medicine. Wants make it better: eating out, subscriptions, new clothes.

Most spending sits in between. When you are unsure, ask what happens if you wait a week.

Emergency fund

This is money set aside for the unexpected: a phone repair, a medical bill, a sudden trip home.

It should be easy to reach and separate from everyday spending. A common target is RM100 of essential expenses.

Debt basics

Borrowing is not automatically bad. The cost is the interest, and how long you carry it.

Minimum payments are built to keep the debt alive. In Malaysia the minimum is usually 5% of the outstanding balance or RM50, whichever is higher — paying more than that shortens the debt.

Questions

Things people ask

Short answers to the questions that come up most.

Do I need to track every sen?

No. Start with the fixed costs and one weekly amount for everything else. If you track too closely at the beginning, you will probably stop. A rough plan you keep is better than a perfect one you abandon.

I have no savings yet. Where do I start?

Move a small fixed amount on payday, before you spend anything. RM20 is a real start. Increase it when a bill disappears or your income rises.

Is 50/30/20 a rule?

It is a guideline, not a law. It gives you a starting point for splitting income between needs, wants and savings. If rent takes more than half your pay, adjust the other two. The Budget page walks through it.

How big should an emergency fund be?

A common target is 6 months of essential expenses. If that feels far away, aim for one month first, then build from there.

How much debt is too much?

A useful measure is the debt service ratio: the share of your monthly income that goes to debt payments. 1% of income

If you are already struggling, talk to your bank early rather than taking another loan to cover the first one.
